Audit Your Own Username Footprint in 15 Minutes

Find the accounts tied to your usernames, see what links them together, and decide what stays.

26 October 20265 min read

Most people have more accounts than they remember: a forum from school, a game profile, a shop account created for one order. Each one may carry the same username, and some carry far more than that. The quickest way to see what others can see is to look yourself. Set aside fifteen minutes and work through these steps.

1. List your handles (2 minutes)

Write down every username you use now and every one you can remember using before. Include the variants: with and without dots or underscores, with a number on the end, and the part of your email address before the @, which many people reuse without thinking about it.

2. Search each one (5 minutes)

Run each handle through PROBE. Note every found result. Open the manual-only links for platforms you know you have used, because those cannot be checked automatically. Treat unknown and blocked as “not checked”, not as “no account”. How to search a username explains each result in more detail.

3. Confirm what is actually yours (3 minutes)

Open each found profile. Some will be yours, some will belong to strangers who chose the same name, and occasionally one will be someone imitating you. Sort them into three piles: mine, not mine, and unsure. Only your own accounts need action. If an account is impersonating you, use the platform's own reporting process rather than confronting the owner.

4. Look for what links your accounts (3 minutes)

A shared username is only the first link. These details make the connection much stronger:

  • the same profile photo or avatar across sites;
  • bios that link to each other or to a personal website;
  • your real name, birth year, home town, school or employer in a handle or bio;
  • old posts that mention where you live or work.

Decide which accounts you are happy to have connected, such as a professional profile and a portfolio, and which you would rather keep apart.

5. Keep, tighten, lock or delete (2 minutes to decide)

  • Keep the accounts you use and are happy to be found by.
  • Tighten the ones you keep but do not want linked: change the photo, trim the bio, remove cross-links, and review privacy settings.
  • Lock accounts you want to keep but not show, if the platform offers private profiles.
  • Delete what you no longer use, through the platform's own account deletion process.

Before deleting, consider whether someone else could later register the same name and be mistaken for you. For a handle that people associate with you, keeping a dormant, private account can be the safer choice.

What deleting does not do

Removing an account changes what the platform shows today. Copies may survive in search engine caches, web archives or other people's screenshots for some time. A not found result on your next search is good news, but it describes the present, not the past.

Make it a habit

Put a reminder in your calendar to repeat the audit every few months, and again after any big change such as a new job or a move. It gets faster each time, because the list from step 1 already exists. For the bigger picture of how handles connect accounts, read What your username doesn't hide.
