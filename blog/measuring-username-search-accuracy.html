Measuring Username Search Accuracy Honestly

A single accuracy percentage cannot describe a system that sometimes refuses to guess.

19 September 20266 min read

Username search is not a conventional database lookup. Every platform presents different status codes, redirects, login walls, rate limits, and bot challenges. A useful evaluation therefore has to measure both whether a decision was correct and how often the system could make one.

The test

We ran the production probe logic against all 324 automatic sources. The campaign included 299 known-positive controls and 648 deterministic, high-entropy negative controls. Another 49 active sources were intentionally manual-only and excluded from automatic scoring.

Responses classified as blocked or unknown counted as abstentions. They did not improve accuracy and they did not count as wrong decisions. That distinction matters: reporting 95.34% without the 72.44% coverage figure would make the system sound more complete than it is.

What changed

The first campaign exposed eight false-positive controls across Kick, Scratch, Brickset, and Greasy Fork. Correcting endpoint selection and source-specific signatures removed all observed false positives. A manual review of twenty apparent false negatives found twelve genuine detection bugs, four stale or deleted controls, and four cases that could not be automated safely.

After focused fixes, positive recall among decided cases rose from 76.82% to 85.05%. The final campaign recorded 182 true positives, 472 true negatives, zero false positives, 32 false negatives, and 261 abstentions.

The defensible claim

When PROBE made a found-or-not-found decision in this campaign, it was correct 95.34% of the time. It made such a decision for 72.44% of controls. Zero observed false positives across 648 synthetic negatives is encouraging, but it is evidence from a finite sample, not proof that false positives are impossible.

This is why the product exposes uncertainty and asks investigators to verify matches before drawing conclusions. Accuracy work is continuous because upstream sites change continuously.
